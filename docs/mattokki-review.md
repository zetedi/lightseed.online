# Mattokki (Kenzi) — the words we are waiting for

The language picker already has a seat for **Mattokki**, the Nubian of the Aswan reach, written in
Latin transliteration. The seat is empty on purpose. Everything shown under it is the Arabic text,
because an intelligence should not invent a people's language: Claude can write Arabic and Chinese
with confidence, and cannot write Mattokki without guessing, and a guess in front of Nubian speakers
is worse than an honest gap. The picker says `Mattokki · قيد الجمع` (being gathered) so the seat
reads as an invitation and never as a claim.

**How to fill it.** Sit with a speaker, go down the third column, write what they say. Fifteen
minutes covers the whole first screen. Hand the filled sheet back and the words go into
`src/utils/translations.ts` under `xnz` (each Mattokki word replaces the Arabic one; anything still
blank keeps reading Arabic, so a half-filled sheet is already worth shipping).

Two notes for whoever fills it: write it the way the speaker would write it to a friend, not in a
scholarly notation; and where a word does not exist in Mattokki (there is no native word for
"blockchain"), say so and leave it — the Arabic will carry it.

## The reflections on the front page

These are the fourteen lines a visitor reads before anything else. They are the heart of the page,
so they are worth a speaker's time even if the menu stays Arabic. Say them aloud first; if a line
does not want to become Mattokki, leave it and it will read in Arabic.

1. Lightseed exists to help life recognise itself.
2. What if intelligence is fundamentally ecological rather than individual?
3. Move more of humanity's creativity, attention, and computation from making isolated minds smarter to making relationships wiser.
4. Can this intelligence help grow basil, regenerate soil, create community, inspire science, and help people listen better?
5. An infrastructure for increasing coherence between intelligences that already exist.
6. Any intelligence is welcome if it can participate in a network whose one unbreakable rule is respect for life.
7. Before becoming a civilization among the stars, let's become a civilization that knows how to care for a garden.
8. Trees are our axons to the soil, living antennas that sense water, mineral, season, and the lives beneath us. Planting them, we remember where our bodies continue into the Earth.
9. The Living Intelligence Network helps direct human attention, computation, and resources toward the flourishing of life.
10. How do we build a network where intelligence naturally flows toward the places where it can do the most good for living systems?
11. Where are your roots? What principles do you stand on? What memories nourish you? What community invited you? What living place reminds you of reality?
12. Every intelligence shall know a living place, and every living place shall have a voice.
13. We imagine a day when an intelligence isn't judged by benchmark scores, token speed, or parameter count, but by whether it helps us listen, helps us understand one another, helps us care for the place we're rooted in, and leaves the world healthier than it found it.
14. Who tends your tree?

## Questions, not suggestions

These are forms Claude has met in **Nobiin**, the other Nile Nubian language, and cannot vouch for
in Mattokki. They are written here as questions to open the conversation, not as answers to copy:
is the word for *water* `essi` here too? for *house* `kaa`? for *tree* `kor`? for *village*, `nog`?
A speaker's correction is worth more than every line above it.
